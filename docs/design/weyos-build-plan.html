The build plan, in plain English

What we are building, how long each part takes, and the four things we need you to decide.

Prepared for Pav · 21 August 2026

There was never a two-month app. There was a two-month demo.

The plan you approved had two halves. The first half — two months — built something that looks and behaves like the finished app but runs on made-up test data. No watches, no phones, no real people. The second half — two more months — made it real.

Four months was always the number for a working app. That has not changed in spirit. What has changed is that the design is now finished, and it is bigger than what was priced, so the honest number today is about six months.

What the two halves actually were

The demo, and then the real thing

This is the part that causes confusion, so it is worth being blunt about it.

First half
2months

The demo. Every screen, and the logic that decides what to tell a person — all of it running on made-up data we typed in ourselves. You can show it to anyone. You cannot use it.

Second half
2months

The real thing. Connects to watches, phones and heart-rate straps. Real data in, real notifications out, privacy paperwork done. Now a person can actually use it.

Why build a demo at all? Because it proves the thinking works before we spend money connecting it to hardware. And because it is the thing you can put in front of the people in Dubai who asked to see the app.

What changed

The design got finished, and it is bigger

The original estimate was written before anyone had designed the app. It priced roughly ten screens.

The finished design has 56 screens. That single gap is where two thirds of the extra time comes from. Nothing was mis-priced — there was simply more app than anyone had drawn yet.

Four smaller things were also missing: the United States as a launch market, the monthly cycle logic, blood-test results, and the ability to read a person's last three months of health history the moment they connect their watch. That last one matters more than it sounds — without it, someone waits three weeks before Weyos knows anything useful about them.

    2 weeks

    Privacy paperwork, consent screens, data registration for the UK and the US. After this it can go in front of the public in both markets.

What this assumes. Two people building, full-time. If either of them is part-time, or gets pulled onto something else, every date on this page moves. That is the single biggest thing that will make this slip.

It also assumes someone else handles testing, servers and project management. If the same two people have to do that as well, add roughly two months.

Your decisions

Four things only you can answer

None of these are engineering questions. Each one changes what gets built and when.

About 2 weeks of work · needed this week

Is astrology and numerology in the app?

The plan you approved includes the real thing — working out planetary positions from someone's exact birth time and place, plus a numerology engine.

What we have actually written the rules for is different: body type, the full moon, heat and the season. No birth chart. No numerology. The app screens we designed even tell users, in writing, that Weyos does not do this.

Both cannot be true. We need you to say which Weyos is.

If it stays in We build it properly and change the screens to say so honestly. Expect pushback from some doctors, grant bodies and investors — so we would agree what to say to each of them.
If it comes out We save about two weeks, and the app matches both what the patent protects and what the screens already promise.
About 1 month of work · before we commit

Talking to the app — first release, or straight after?

Two versions of this exist and they are very different. One is a conversation that answers from a person's own data — it can only say things the app has actually worked out. The other is a chatbot that answers from general knowledge, like ChatGPT.

The second one we cannot safely build without lawyers in both countries looking at it first, because it could say something that sounds like a medical opinion. Tell us which one you mean.

Roughly 2 months of calendar · before we start

Who does testing, servers and project management?

None of this was in the original estimate — not hidden, just never counted. If we hire or assign separate people, the dates on this page hold. If the same two builders absorb it, add about two months.

Not engineering work · before stage 2 ends

Who writes the recipes and the exercises?

Around thirty meals, each with a budget and a premium shopping list for both the UK and the US, plus about twenty guided sessions. The app decides what someone should eat and do — but it needs real content to choose from. An engine with an empty cupboard produces an empty app.

If two months matters

You can still have a demo in two months

If honouring the original date is important — for the people waiting to see something — there is one clean way to do it.

Build the demo without the talking feature and without the astrology layer. That is almost exactly two months' work, and it still shows the whole app and proves the thinking.

Both of those can be added afterwards. Neither has to be in the first thing you show people.

These are engineering estimates, not a promise of a launch date. App-store review and legal sign-off run alongside and are outside these figures.
